Hybrid search and reranking

Two searches that fail on different questions, run together, then a second, slower model that sorts the combined results. "Retrieve wide, rerank narrow" is the standard shape of production retrieval.

The problem

Each search has a blind spot

Vector search6511/12
Keyword search167/12

Real users send both kinds of question, and you cannot know which is coming. Picking one search means accepting its blind spot. The question is how to combine two result lists whose scores are not on the same scale: a cosine of 0.72 and a BM25 score of 5.13 cannot be added.

The picture

Retrieve wide, rerank narrow

  1. Question
  2. Vector searchby meaning
    Keyword searchby exact words
  3. Fusemerge the two lists by rank
  4. Reranka second model scores each candidate against the question
  5. Top fewinto the prompt

The two searches are cheap and cast a wide net: their job is that the right chunk is somewhere among the candidates. The reranker is slow and precise: its job is to put it first.

How it works

Fusion, then a judge

Reciprocal rank fusion (RRF)

RRF ignores the scores and uses only positions. Each list gives a chunk 1 ÷ (60 + rank); a chunk's total is the sum over the lists. Being near the top of either list is good; being near the top of both is best.

score(chunk) = Σ over the lists 1 / (60 + rank of the chunk in that list)

60 is the customary constant. It keeps the gap between rank 1 and rank 2 small, so one list cannot dominate.

The reranker chooses

A reranker is a small model called a cross-encoder. It differs from an embedding model in one way that matters:

Embedding model (chapter 5)Cross-encoder (reranker)
ReadsThe question alone, each chunk alone. Compares two vectors afterwards.The question and one chunk together, word against word.
SpeedChunk vectors are computed once and indexed. Search is instant.One model run per candidate, at question time. Nothing can be precomputed.
So you use itTo search everything.To sort the 10 to 50 candidates the search found.

Reading the pair together, it can see that the question says 140 and the chunk says 401:

Tied for first.

  1. 6 FIN-140 2.69

The right chunk first; the look-alike last.

Reranker scores are raw model outputs and can be negative. Only their order means anything.

Use it when, skip it when

What each stage buys you

Add it when

  • Hybrid: your evals show vector search missing exact terms, or keyword search missing paraphrases.
  • Reranking: the right chunk is among the candidates but not at the top, or you want to send fewer chunks to the model.
  • Both: a mixed audience asking mixed questions. This is the common production set-up.

Skip it when

  • One search already passes your evals. Every stage is more code, more latency, more to tune.
  • Reranking: the response-time budget is tight. It adds a model run per candidate.
  • You have no eval yet. Without a score you cannot tell whether a stage helped.

from fastembed.rerank.cross_encoder import TextCrossEncoder

def fuse(*result_lists):
    """Reciprocal rank fusion: each list gives a chunk 1 / (60 + its rank). Sum, then sort."""
    totals = {}
    for results in result_lists:
        for rank, (chunk_id, _) in enumerate(results, start=1):
            totals[chunk_id] = totals.get(chunk_id, 0) + 1 / (60 + rank)
    return sorted(totals.items(), key=lambda pair: pair[1], reverse=True)

reranker = TextCrossEncoder("Xenova/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2")    # reads the question and one chunk together

def rerank(question, candidates):
    ids = [chunk_id for chunk_id, _ in candidates]
    scores = reranker.rerank(question, [CHUNKS[i - 1]["text"] for i in ids])
    return sorted(zip(ids, map(float, scores)), key=lambda pair: pair[1], reverse=True)

def retrieve(question):
    candidates = fuse(vector_search(question), keyword_search(question))
    return [CHUNKS[chunk_id - 1] for chunk_id, _ in rerank(question, candidates)[:3]]

Two honest readings. First: fusion on its own made first place worse than vector search alone, 8 against 11, because every disagreement between the lists became a tie or a wrong winner. What fusion guarantees is the second column: the right chunk is always a candidate. The reranker turns that into the first column.

Second: this is 12 questions over 14 chunks. The numbers show the mechanism; they are not a benchmark. On your data the gaps will differ, which is why you keep your own scoreboard.
