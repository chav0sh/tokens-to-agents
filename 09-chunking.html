Chunking and indexing

So far the handbook arrived neatly cut into 14 chunks. Real documents are PDFs, wiki pages and long policies. How you cut them decides what the search can find, and it is the least glamorous, most underrated part of a RAG system.

The problem

Too big is blurry, too small is meaningless

ChunkWhat goes wrong
A whole documentOne vector for forty topics is a blur that matches nothing well. It may exceed the embedding model's input limit and be cut off silently. And it fills the prompt with text the question does not need.
A single sentence"It must be requested 30 days in advance." What must? The chunk lost the sentence before it, so neither the search nor the model can use it.
Cut at a fixed lengthThe cut lands mid-sentence or between a rule and its exception. Each half is retrieved without the other.

A good chunk is one idea, complete enough to make sense on its own.

The picture

The indexing pipeline

  1. LoadPDF, HTML, wiki, tickets
  2. Cleanto plain text; drop menus, footers, page numbers
  3. Splitinto chunks of one idea
  4. Labeltitle, section, date, who may read it
  5. Embed and indexvectors + keywords

A batch job, like any import: it runs when documents change, not when a question arrives. Everything a search can ever return was decided here.

How it works

Ways to split, and what they cost

StrategyHowGood for
By structureUse the document's own units: a heading and its paragraph, one rule, one Q&A pair, one function.Anything with structure. The best option when you have it.
RecursiveSplit by section; if a piece is still too long, by paragraph; then by sentence.Mixed documents. The usual default in libraries.
Fixed size with overlapEvery N tokens, each chunk repeating the last few sentences of the one before.Text with no structure at all: transcripts, logs.

Overlap means neighbouring chunks share a little text, so a sentence cut at a boundary survives whole in at least one of them.

The size trade-off

Smaller chunksLarger chunks
SearchSharper: one topic per vectorBlurrier: several topics averaged
Context for the modelMay miss the surrounding sentencesCarries its own context
In the promptMore chunks fitFewer fit
IndexMore vectors to store and searchFewer

A few hundred tokens per chunk is a common starting point. There is no best size: it depends on your documents and questions, and the way to find it is to change it and rerun your scoreboard.

Worked: a 200-page handbook

200 pages ≈ 100,000 words ≈ 133,000 tokens (100 tokens per 75 words).
Chunks of 400 tokens with 50 overlapping: a new chunk every 350 tokens → 133,000 ÷ 350 ≈ 381 chunks.
Stored vectors: 381 × 1,536 bytes ≈ 0.6 MB.
Per question, 3 chunks go into the prompt: 1,200 tokens instead of 133,000.

The whole handbook is small for a database and large for a prompt. That gap is why retrieval exists.

Labels: what you store next to the text

LabelUsed for
Document title, sectionThe citation in the answer. Also worth putting into the chunk's text, so the search and the model know where a lone paragraph came from.
Source link or IDLetting the user open the original.
Date, versionPreferring the current policy over last year's.
Who may read itFiltering the search per user. Permissions belong in the query (chapter 6), never in the prompt.

Keeping the index fresh

The index is a copy of your documents, so it goes stale like any cache. Store a hash of each document: when it changes, delete that document's chunks and index it again. Two events force a full rebuild: changing the embedding model, and changing how you split.

Use it when, skip it when

How much effort to spend here

Spend time on chunking when

  • Documents are long, or mix many topics.
  • The scoreboard shows the right passage is never retrieved, or arrives cut in half.
  • Sources are messy: PDFs with tables, scanned pages, HTML full of navigation.

Skip it when

  • Your data is already small records: FAQ entries, tickets, product descriptions. One record, one chunk.
  • The whole corpus fits in the prompt. Then there is nothing to split.

In the docs assistant

The handbook is split by its own structure

handbook.md has a heading per policy and one rule per paragraph. So: one chunk per paragraph, labelled with its heading.

def load_chunks() -> list[dict]:
    """One chunk per paragraph, each carrying the title of its section."""
    chunks, title = [], ""
    for block in re.split(r"\n\s*\n", HANDBOOK.read_text(encoding="utf-8").strip()):
        if block.startswith("# "):
            title = block[2:].strip()                       # a heading: remember it
        else:
            chunks.append({"id": len(chunks) + 1, "doc": title, "text": " ".join(block.split())})
    return chunks
{'id': 1, 'doc': 'Leave policy', 'text': 'Employees get 12 weeks of paid parental leave after the birth or adoption of a child.'} {'id': 2, 'doc': 'Leave policy', 'text': 'Parental leave must be requested 30 days in advance using form HR-2291.'} …

The doc label is what lets the answer end with "[Security policy]". The index is rebuilt from the file each time the app starts: with 14 chunks that takes a moment and can never be stale.
